---
title: Emergency stop
description: Cut access at once for a person, a device, a service, an environment or everyone, and restore it later.
---

The **Emergency stop** page (under **Security** in the sidebar) cuts access immediately during an incident: a lost
laptop, a leaked key or someone who should no longer connect. Live database and API connections close within
seconds and new ones are refused until you restore access. Owners and admins see this page.

An [emergency stop](/docs/overview/concepts#emergency-stop) doesn't delete anything. Grants, variables and devices
stay as they are, so restoring access puts everything back.

<Frame src="/docs/images/guides/emergency-stop.png" alt="Stop access dialog with the five scopes and the Reason field" caption="Stop access dialog with the five scopes and the Reason field" />

## Scopes

| Option in **What to stop** | Stops | Pick the target |
|---|---|---|
| **A person** | Every device of one member | **Person** |
| **A device** | One laptop, for example lost or stolen | **Device** |
| **A resource** | One database or API | **In environment**, then **Service** |
| **An environment** | Everything in one environment | **Environment** |
| **Everyone** | The whole organization, admins too | Nothing to pick |

For help choosing, see [Use the kill switch](/docs/guides/admin/kill-switch).

## Stop access

<Steps>
<Step>
### Open the dialog

Select **Stop access** at the top of the page.
</Step>
<Step>
### Choose what to stop

Pick a scope under **What to stop**, then choose the target.
</Step>
<Step>
### Type a reason

Enter a **Reason** of at least three characters, for example `laptop reported stolen`. It is shown to admins and
recorded in [Activity](/docs/guides/dashboard/audit).
</Step>
<Step>
### Confirm

Select **Stop access** (or **Stop access for everyone** for the whole organization).
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="danger" title="Everyone means everyone">
**Everyone** stops every app in the organization, including those of owners and admins. Use it only when you
suspect a breach you can't contain with a narrower scope.
</Callout>

## What developers see

Within seconds of a stop:

- Database clients get a protocol-native error that reads `cb: access revoked by admin`.
- HTTP calls through the gateway get `403` with `{ "error": "cb_access_revoked" }`.
- A running `cb run` prints a banner starting with `cb: access revoked by admin` and leaves the app running, so it
  surfaces its own errors.
- A new `cb run` refuses to start, and the app is never given any real credentials.

## Restore access

Active stops are listed under **Active**, with the target, a scope badge (**Person**, **Device**, **Service**,
**Environment** or **Everyone**), the reason and how long ago it started. Choose **Restore access** in the row's
"…" menu and confirm. People with access can connect again, and running apps reconnect automatically.

Restored stops move to **History**, which keeps the most recent ones with their reason and when access was restored.
